# The Hunt — one page before you start

## The gist

You're playing a card game against one opponent, over and over, down a road of 25 of them. Each of
you has a health bar. You never heal during a fight. **Every trick you get wrong costs you 1 health,
and that's the only thing that can kill you.**

## The deal

Three suits — Bells, Keys, Moons — numbered 1 to 11. You get six cards, so does the opponent, and one
extra card is turned face up: **its suit is trump for the whole hand.**

Six tricks. You each play one card. **You must follow the led suit if you have it**; if you don't,
play anything. Highest trump wins; failing that, highest card of the led suit wins.

Your hand tops back up to four cards as you go. Theirs doesn't.

## The one rule that makes this game different: skulls

About two of the opponent's six cards carry a **skull**. A skull flips what winning is worth.

|                     | Normal trick   | Skulled trick  |
| ------------------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| **You take it**     | good — you score | **bad — 1 health** |
| **You don't take it** | **bad — 1 health** | good — you score |

So on a skulled trick you are trying to **lose**. Playing a 2 under their skulled 5 is the correct
play, not a surrender.

You can't see which of their cards are skulled while they're in hand — but the panel tells you, **for
each suit, how many they hold and how many of those are skulled.** Read that before you lead. And the
moment they play a skulled card, its face is a skull, so you can see it before you answer.

## Scoring — you choose when to cash out

Every good trick adds to two numbers: a **total** (damage) and a **roll** (how many good tricks in a
row). What you're sitting on is **total × roll** — so three in a row is worth 9, six in a row is 36.

After every good trick the game asks: **apply**, or **roll over**. Apply deals it to the opponent and
resets you to zero. Roll over keeps building.

**One bad trick takes all of it and gives them nothing.** So rolling over is a real bet.

## Three rules that will save you

1. **Decide the trick before you arm anything.** Look at the suit panel, pick the suit the trick will
   be played in and whether you're trying to take it or duck it — then only arm buff cards that can
   pay under that plan. Arming a Bells card on a trick you'll play in Keys wastes it.
2. **Never arm a "win with X" card and a "lose with X" card on the same trick.** Exactly one of them
   can pay.
3. **Save your Cheat.** A Cheat lets you ignore follow-suit. Its one good moment is when they lead a
   skull and every legal card you hold would win it — the Cheat lets you throw something off-suit and
   duck it instead. Don't spend it as an ordinary buff.

One more, if you want the numbers to get silly: **firing several buff cards on the same trick pays
much more than firing them one at a time.**

## Card abilities

Only the odd-numbered cards do anything.

| Card | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **1 — Swan** | Whoever *loses* the trick leads the next one. Your only way to hand the lead back. |
| **3 — Fox** | You may swap a card from your hand with the face-up trump card — changing trump, this trick included. |
| **5 — Woodcutter** | Draw the top card, then bury any one card from your hand. |
| **9 — Witch** | Counts as trump — unless both of you play one, in which case neither does. |
| **11 — Monarch** | If it's *led*, the follower must answer with their 1 of that suit or their highest of that suit. |

7 and 8 do nothing at all.

## Two things to know that aren't your fault

- **Whoever wins a trick leads the next one.** Leading is where the danger is, because you're guessing
  what they'll answer with. That's the point, not a bug.
- **The shop's "combine two cards into one better one" screen currently makes you weaker.** Ignore it.

## What we want to know

Not whether you won. Whether the skull flip clicked, whether you ever enjoyed deliberately losing a
trick, whether the suit panel felt like information or like homework, and where you were bored.
