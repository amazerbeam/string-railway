# Steam page

**The game is called *The Long Wake*** (decided 2026-09-04). `.docs/design/tech-duinn-lore.md` §1 owns
the name and why it was chosen; this page just uses it. The one thing to carry over here: **the title
does not say "card game"**, so the short description has to, every time.

Store page copy in progress, and the trailer beat sheet. **The page is not up yet** — the trigger is
one honest-looking screen in the Unity build (`content-plan.md` §5). These drafts exist so that when
the trigger fires the page goes up in a day, not a month.

Nothing here is final. The short description in particular should be redrafted many times; it is the
hardest sentence in the project.

## Short description — drafts

The job: genre and twist, in one sentence a stranger can repeat to a friend.

1. A trick-taking roguelike played against the dead, where the cards that beat you are the ones you
   were never meant to win.
2. Sit down at a wake in the house of the dead and play cards for passage. Some tricks you win by
   losing.
3. A roguelike card game where winning a trick is sometimes exactly how you die.
4. Play cards with the dead for the right to pass on. Read the omens, take the tricks you should,
   and duck the ones you shouldn't.

Draft 3 is the sharpest and the most misleading — it undersells the deckbuilding entirely. Draft 1
carries both halves. Test them on someone who has not seen the game.

## The hook, stated plainly

For anywhere that needs a paragraph rather than a sentence:

> You are newly dead, waiting in Tech Duinn — the house where Irish tradition says the dead gather
> before they pass onward. The way people wait around the dead is a wake: drinking, stories and
> games. So you play cards. Three suits — the Raven, the Salmon, the Hound — against one opponent,
> down a road of twenty-five of them. Every trick you get wrong costs you a life you no longer have
> much of. And some of their cards carry an omen, which flips the whole thing: on those, taking the
> trick is what kills you, and throwing it away is how you score.

## Long description — structure, not copy

In order:

1. The wake and the table — the fiction, three sentences.
2. **What you actually do** — follow suit, six tricks, highest trump takes it. Keep this concrete;
   people need to know it is a real card game with real rules.
3. **The omen** — the inversion, with the worked example: they lead a skulled 5, you play a 2 under
   it, and that is correct. This is the section that sells the game.
4. **The bet** — every good trick builds a running total and a streak, and the pot is the two
   multiplied. Cash out, or roll it over. One bad trick takes all of it.
5. **Charms** — the small conditional objects you carry, armed for a single trick and paid when that
   trick resolves. This is the run-to-run building block.
6. **The Cairn** — what survives a failed run, framed as it is in the fiction: a stone added to a
   pile by everyone who passes.

## Tags — candidates

Genre-honest, per the playbook. Card Game, Deckbuilding, Roguelike, Trick-Taking, Turn-Based
Strategy, Singleplayer, Dark Fantasy, Mythology. Avoid anything that puts the page in front of an
action-roguelike audience that will bounce.

## Screenshots — the five that have to exist

One per part of the loop, all in-game, none staged:

1. The table mid-trick, with the opponent's card face up.
2. The moment an omen is showing and the correct play is the low card.
3. The score mid-roll-over, both numbers high, with the cash-out choice on screen.
4. Charms being armed for a trick.
5. The Cairn — what carried over from the last run.

## Trailer beat sheet

1. **Gameplay immediately** — the most legible five seconds of a trick. No logo first.
2. **The hook** — the omen inversion, shown, not narrated. This is the beat the whole trailer exists
   for; give it room.
3. **Escalation** — three or four cuts: a run building, charms firing together, a pot getting silly,
   a near-death reversal.
4. **The best moment** — the biggest single hit the game has produced, held slightly longer than is
   comfortable.
5. **Title card, then the wishlist prompt.** In that order, at the end, briefly.

No voiceover unless the developer wants to be in it. No text explaining the rules — if a rule needs
explaining, show its consequence instead. The omen beat has to work on sight alone; if it doesn't,
the trailer is wrong, not the viewer.
