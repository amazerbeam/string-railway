The only one of the four where you are on camera. It is the channel's thesis stated once, properly,
so that everything posted for the next year has a reason behind it.

**This pack replaced a clip about renaming the game.** That clip would have announced a setting and a
new vocabulary that are, as of 2026-09-04, proposals in three uncommitted documents. Announcing a
setting is unrecallable, and if it changes the earliest part of the audience has been taught
something false. When the setting is actually decided, it is worth a video of its own — a good one.
Not before.

## Wording that is fixed

- **"A prototype answers one question: is this fun?"** That is the whole argument and the line worth
  getting clean. Everything else in the clip is support for it.
- **"Throwing it away isn't failure — it's the plan."** Viewers will read a rebuild as something
  going wrong. Say plainly that it was always the intent, or the clip reads as a setback.
- **Do not name the setting or any noun that is being renamed.** Say "the opponent", "a skull",
  "damage". The mechanics are stable; the names are not.
- **Do not promise a date.** You have an empty project. A timeline stated now is a stick to be beaten
  with later.

## Notes for the editor

- Talking head, so crop tight to the speaker rather than letterboxing with a blurred backdrop. Check
  the top of the head on an extracted frame — that is what the crop loses.
- Cutaways should be short, two seconds each, and should return to the face. The face is the point of
  this one.
- Caption the fixed lines in full. They are the most quotable thing in the pack.
- If the take rambles past 45 seconds but stays good, keep the longer version and cut a second,
  tighter one. Both are postable.

## Follow-ups this unlocks

The honest-process format is the durable one for this channel: what the prototype proved, what it got
wrong, what the rebuild changes. One per idea, all of them verifiable against the code, none of them
dependent on a decision that has not been made.
