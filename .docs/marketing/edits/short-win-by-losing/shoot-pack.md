# Shoot pack — Sometimes winning is how you lose

**Slug:** `short-win-by-losing` · **Target:** vertical 9:16 · **Captions:** yes, burned
**Length:** 30–40 seconds · **Media folder:** `D:\Recordings\StringsAndStations\short-win-by-losing\`

The most important clip of the four. It is the one rule that makes this not a normal trick-taker,
and if a viewer understands only one thing about the game, it should be this.

It is also the safest clip to make right now, because the skull is one of the few things that is
**not** being renamed — the port keeps the word and the icon deliberately, because a skull tells a
player to avoid something on sight with no teaching required. Everything you say here survives the
port.

---

## Capture

**One live play session, talking the whole way through.** Do not capture silently and narrate later,
and do not stop to stage a hand unless nothing usable turns up — roughly a third of the opponent's
cards carry a skull, so the situation arrives on its own within a few hands.

Say what you are about to do *before* you do it. The decision is the content; the card landing is
just the proof.

What has to end up on screen at some point:

1. Their skulled card, led, with your hand showing both options.
2. You playing the **low** card — no health lost, the score banking.
3. **The comparison shot:** the same situation, high card played, you eat the skull and lose a health
   point.

**Talk while you capture and call the keepers out loud** — "that's the one", "ignore that". That
spoken marker is how the editor finds the right ten seconds in a long take.

## Wording that is fixed

- **"Playing low there isn't giving up. It's the correct play."** Every card player's instinct is
  that ducking a trick is defensive. Naming it *correct* is the whole reframe.
- **"I didn't take the trick, and I still scored."** Not "I won" — taking and winning have come
  apart, and that is the point.
- **Avoid the word "win" in this clip entirely.** The game's name for this outcome is a **Low
  Victory** — you went low, the outcome was a Victory. You do not need that term on camera, but
  never call it "winning a trick": that means something different here and the confusion carries
  forward.

## Notes for the editor

- The comparison shot is the clip's engine: same situation, two choices, one costs health. Cut them
  back to back, tight, no transition.
- Caption both outcomes plainly — *"took it → lost health"* / *"ducked it → scored"*. Most of this
  gets watched with the sound off and the idea has to survive that.
- Hold on the moment the health does **not** drop. That non-event is the payoff and it needs a beat
  longer than feels natural.
- Nothing gets captioned as "won the trick". Check the burned text before rendering.
- Say "the opponent". Do not use the code's name for them — it is being renamed and nothing is
  settled.
