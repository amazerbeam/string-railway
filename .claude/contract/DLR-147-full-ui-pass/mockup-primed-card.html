A primed card — the Timebomb mark

What a card looks like once a Timebomb has been primed onto it. Scope is the mark only, not the arming flow. The ruleset requires the mark wherever the card renders — in hand, in the trick, in an ability prompt, and on the decree if the Fox moves it there — and requires it to be part of the card's spoken name, so it never depends on seeing the glyph.

Your hand, with the 7 of Bells primed

One card carries the bomb. Everything else about it is unchanged — it is still the 7 of Bells, still legal or illegal on its own merits, still played the same way.

7 OF BELLS IS PRIMED — IT CARRIES +4 TO WHOEVER WINS ITS TRICK

The mark, close up

A cartoon bomb, sat half over the card's top-right corner so it reads as an object placed on the card rather than as part of its printing. That is the distinction worth holding: a skull replaces the art, because a skull is what the card is this trick — a Timebomb is added, because it is a temporary thing you did to a card that is otherwise still itself.

What this proposes

  • A bomb, not a glyph. Black body with a rim light and a specular, a short fuse, and a spark that fizzes. It sits at the top-right corner, half off the card, which puts it in the one part of a card that stays visible if the hand is ever overlapped or fanned.
  • The green ring stays. --wc-timebomb already drives --wc-hp-ticking-fill, the ticking hearts on the health bar, so green means "a Timebomb is involved" everywhere else in the UI. The bomb is the icon; the green is the system colour, and the ring is what ties this card to the damage you can already see coming on the bar. Dropping it would break that thread.
  • Added, not substituted. The card keeps its rank, suit, name and art. A primed Swan is still a Swan. This is deliberately the opposite treatment from a skull.
  • The fizz is one small looping animation, on a `steps()` timing so it flickers rather than glides. It stops dead under prefers-reduced-motion, where the spark simply sits lit — the mark still reads, it just stops moving.
  • It survives greyscale: the bomb is a black silhouette against a pale card, and the shape alone carries it. The green ring is reinforcement, never the only signal.
  • The name carries it too — "7 of Bells — Treasure, primed" — as the ruleset requires.

Open — yours, or a rules call

  • A card can be both skulled and primed. Shown in the third close-up. The bomb sits over a skull card without much trouble at this size, but it is two loud marks on one small object and worth a look at hand scale before committing.
  • Should the bomb show its tier? A gold Timebomb hits for 12/6 and a bronze for 4/2, and the mark currently says nothing about which. The ruleset also records that priming a second card overwrites the first's tier — so if the mark ever did show a figure, two primed cards could both be lying.
  • The bomb's size, its overhang, the fuse, the spark colour and the 620ms fizz are all PLACEHOLDERS.
