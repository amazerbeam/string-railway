Cheat & Timebomb on the buff screen

The only two utility cards in the game — the only two you press. Everything else in your loadout waits for a condition to come true. This sheet is about how they should appear in the buff picker: how you find them, how you tell them apart from the passive cards, and what their tier means. It is not about what happens after you spend one.

The two cards, at every tier

Both are PRESS cadence and neither targets a suit, so both land in the picker's suitless run. Tier means something different on each: a Cheat's tier is duration, a Timebomb's tier is damage — the two figures scale on completely different axes, and the card face is the only place that difference can be read.

Where they sit in the picker

The gallery groups by target suit. Today these two fall into “No suit” alongside Sidestep — which is wrong for choosing, because Sidestep fires on its own and these two do not. Below: the run as it is now, and the same run split so the pressable cards have their own tab.

Their resting state is “not now”

A Cheat or a Timebomb can only be spent between tricks, so most of the time you open the picker they are in the fenced group at the foot. That means the fence is where a player mostly meets these two cards — worth designing for rather than treating as an error state.

What this proposes

  • A fourth run in the picker: “Press”. The gallery orders by suit, and its last group is currently “No target suit” — a bucket holding both Sidestep (which fires on its own) and these two (which you spend). Those are different kinds of card and a player scanning for “what can I actually do right now” has to read the cadence pill on every one. Splitting the run costs one grid cell and answers the question at a glance. The tab is deliberately solid rather than dashed, because unlike the other three it names an action, not an absence.
  • The PRESS pill is raised. Every other cadence — WIN, LOSE, DODGE, WHEN, HAND END — describes a thing that happens to you. PRESS is the only one that is a verb you perform, so it is the only one drawn as a chip with a lit top edge instead of a flat tint.
  • The Timebomb's payoff bar is split. Its reward as written is +4 damage, and that is only half the card: the same figure is 2 to you if the hit lands on your side. A single bar showing only the upside makes the card look strictly better than it is, at the one moment a player is deciding whether to take it. Green for what it pays, alarm for what it can cost. The Cheat needs no split — its downside is only that it may expire unused.
  • The poised label says what the tap does. “TAP AGAIN TO SPEND”, not “activate”: for these two the second tap is irreversible and the card is consumed, and the ruleset is explicit that Escape works before it and never after.
  • Fenced is tarnish, not absence. They keep their frame and name, desaturated — because the fence is their normal home, and a player needs to recognise what is sitting there.

Open — yours, or a rules call

  • Should “Press” be a run, or a filter? The picker already has a tier filter rail. A fourth chip there — “pressable now” — would answer the same question without spending a grid cell, but it hides the answer behind an interaction instead of showing it.
  • Two Timebomb figures, one card. Priming a second card overwrites the first's tier (recorded as a known tension in the-hunt.md). If that stands, a gold and a bronze Timebomb in the picker are advertising figures that will not both be honoured.
  • The damage numbers (4/2, 8/4, 12/6) and the Cheat durations (1/2/3) are transcribed and marked [provisional] in the ruleset. Every colour and size here is a PLACEHOLDER.
