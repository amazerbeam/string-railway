The trick — a skulled card, and what it does to you

Two changes to the middle of the felt. A skulled card shows a skull instead of its character art — one skull, one wash, identical on every rank and every suit, with the suit still named in the corner index. And underneath the trick sits a readout of what their card actually does to you, which replaces the “Their intent” panel in the left dossier.

The skulled card

Every skulled card is the same object: the same bone skull on the same dark wash, whatever its rank or suit. That uniformity is the point — a skull is a property of the trick, not a character, and a player should recognise one across the table without reading it. The rank and suit stay in the corner, because the trick is still decided on them.

The trick, with the readout underneath

The readout says what their card does to you, in both branches, and never predicts which branch happens — the-hunt.md is explicit that the game will not tell you whether you are about to win, because that would hand you the Quarry's card. So both outcomes are stated and neither is emphasised.

The same readout, for other cards they might lead

The wording follows the rank's real rule — and where a card has nothing to say, the readout does not render at all. No panel appears to tell you that nothing is happening. The two cases below marked “no readout” show that emptiness deliberately; in the game that space is simply felt.

The new requirement, stated plainly

  • A skulled card renders a skull, not its character art. One bone skull on one dark wash, identical across every rank and suit. The corner index keeps rank, suit glyph and rank name, because the trick is still won on rank and suit. The current felt marks a skull with a small glyph on an otherwise ordinary card; this makes it the card's whole face.
  • Below the trick, a readout of the led card's consequence for you — an if you win / if you lose pair when the outcome differs, or a single rule row when the card constrains your legal play or changes how the trick resolves.
  • It renders only when there is something to say. A clean low card produces no panel at all, and neither does the state before the Quarry has played. The readout sits under the trick on every hand, so a row reading “nothing extra” would be permanent furniture teaching the player to stop looking — which is exactly the thing that would make it useless on the hand where it matters.
  • It is an off-white slip, matching the cards — same parchment gradient, same printed hairline, so it reads as part of the deck rather than a HUD panel bolted onto the table.
  • Its text colours are measured, not picked. On a light ground both project accent tokens fail WCAG AA outright — --wc-alarm falls to 3.03:1 and --wc-brass to 2.29:1. Each is replaced by a darker member of its own family so it still reads as the red one and the gold one: body #1b1710 15.96:1, label #5f5647 6.46:1, costs you #96301f 6.86:1, worth having #6f5412 6.36:1. The values are placeholders; the 4.5:1 floor is not.
  • It is deliberately quiet. Labels are plain small-caps, no chips, and colour appears only on the consequence itself. An earlier pass used red and green pills and read as a traffic light — and the green was not a project colour at all.
  • It never predicts the outcome. Both branches are shown, neither highlighted. This is not a style choice: the ruleset withholds the Quarry's card deliberately, and a readout that leaned one way would leak it.
  • “Their intent” is removed from the dossier. Its job was to telegraph what they would lead; once they have led, the card itself is better information, and this readout says what the card means rather than what suit was coming.

The gap that removing “Their intent” leaves — needs your call

  • When you lead, there is no played card to describe. Their card is face down, and the ruleset says a skull or Timebomb mark is not yet decided in that state — which is exactly when a telegraph was worth having. Options, and I have not chosen: the readout carries the telegraph in that state instead (“They are holding Keys”), or it states the estimate the damage readout already uses (slanted, prefixed ~), or it collapses and the felt shows nothing until they answer. The mockup shows the third for comparison.
  • A skull that changes hands keeps its skull — the Quarry's Fox can exchange a skulled card into the decree, and your Fox can take it into hand. So a card in your hand can be skulled, and it must then render as a skull card too. Worth confirming that is wanted, because it means your own hand can contain a card whose face is a skull.
  • Every colour and size here is a PLACEHOLDER — the skull wash, the bone tone, the readout's row colours, and the skull's proportions.
