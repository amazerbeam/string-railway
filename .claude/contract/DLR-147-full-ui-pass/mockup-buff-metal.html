Buff card — the design

The settled buff card: an off-white face, tier carried entirely by a metallic frame — bronze, silver, gold — and the target suit carried by its own glyph in its own colour. With the face neutral, the suit mark and the payoff bar are the only saturated things on the card, so Bells no longer competes with the Bronze field: tier and suit stop fighting for the same channel.

These are all thirteen buffs in the game, not a selection — DLR-145 pared the mintable pool to Taker, Feeder and Sidestep crossed with Blade and Momentum, plus Cheat and Timebomb. That makes the suit question far more load-bearing than it first looked: nine of the thirteen target a suit, and only Sidestep, Cheat and Timebomb do not.

The three metals

Each is a five-stop gradient rather than a tint — a light roll, a mid, a dark core, a specular return, then a shadowed edge — with a bright streak across the top-left and a dark roll on the bottom-right, so the frame has two lit faces instead of one flat colour.

How the card works

  • Tier and suit stop competing. The problem the earlier sheets kept hitting was that Bells and the Bronze field sit 28.7 RGB units apart — effectively the same amber. A neutral face removes the contest: tier becomes material and suit becomes colour, so neither has to lose.
  • Tier is the metallic frame, plus a roman numeral. I, II, III, where a cluster of unlabelled dots used to sit. The dots read as noise rather than as a rank; a numeral is something you can say out loud and it stays shape-distinct in greyscale — which matters, because a metallic gradient reads as light-and-dark in greyscale, never as bronze/silver/gold. The metal alone was never going to carry tier.
  • Suit is the dominant axis in the live pool. Nine of the thirteen mintable buffs are Takers or Feeders, and every one of those targets a suit; only Sidestep, Cheat and Timebomb do not. An earlier sheet guessed "roughly half have no suit" from the full BuffKind list — that list includes eight cut families, and counting it overstated the suitless case badly. Showing suit clearly is worth more than it first appeared.
  • Suit is the bare glyph in its own colour — amber bell, blue key, purple moon. A filled medallion was tried and cut: a disc rendered the glyph at 11px inside a 17px circle, which is not enough room for a bell to look like a bell. Bare, it is 20px — 90% larger — at full stroke weight. The suit sections already do the long-range scanning, so the mark is free to be legible rather than loud.
  • The payoff bar carries the suit colour too, so suit is stated twice: by the mark at the top and the reward at the foot. The text colour on it was measured, not chosen — white fails WCAG AA on all three suits at this size (Bells 2.99:1, Keys 3.37:1, Moons 3.51:1), so each bar takes a dark ink from its own suit family instead: 6.22, 4.92 and 5.14:1. Buffs with no target suit keep the neutral ink bar, which makes that group visibly different.
  • The face is tinted to its own metal — warm cream under bronze, cool paper-white under silver, pale straw under gold. Held close to white on purpose: a saturated face would start competing with the suit mark again, which is the problem this design exists to avoid.
  • Duplicates are a pile, the layers behind carrying the same metal gradient as the frame, so three bronze reads as three bronze-edged cards rather than as a drop shadow. One copy stays a plain card.
  • It matches the playing cards. Both decks are off-white with a printed hairline and a dark-to-coloured bar at the foot, so a buff and a card of the hand read as objects from the same game.
  • Cost: the face lost its tier colour, which is what used to make a full gallery scan as three colour blocks. Ordering does that work now — which is why the toggle is still here.

Open — still yours

  • Every metal stop, the 5px frame width, the specular angle and opacity, the suit-mark size, the three face tints and the off-white itself are PLACEHOLDERS. The suit colours are too — but the contrast floor on the payoff bar is not. Retune a suit colour and its bar text has to be re-measured, because the failing option is the one that looks obvious.
  • Is the tier word at the foot still earning its place? With the numeral, the metal and the tinted face, it is a fourth carrier of the same fact. It is the first thing I would cut.
  • The rim sheen is in. Hovering sweeps a band of light along the frame. It sits below the face in the stacking order, so the light only ever crosses the rim — the face masks the rest for free, with no extra element and no clip path. It fires once per hover, not on a loop: fifteen looping cards would be a fairground. Under prefers-reduced-motion it does not travel, and the rim simply brightens instead, so the hover still reads. Duration, angle, width and brightness are placeholders.
  • Silver reads weakest, being a pale metal on a pale face where bronze and gold have more contrast. Inherent to the palette rather than a bug, but worth checking at gallery scale.
  • A tension to resolve later: the payoff bar is about the reward, and colouring it by suit conflates two facts. If buffs ever need reward-type colour — damage vs coins vs multiplier — this bar is the slot that would want it, and suit would move back to the mark alone.
