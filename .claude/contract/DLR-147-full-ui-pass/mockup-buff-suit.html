Buff cards — showing the target suit

Four ways to make a buff's target suit readable at a glance, each rendering the same ten samples. Only three buff families actually target a suit — Taker, Feeder and Keepsake. Mark of the rank targets a rank, not a suit, and everything else targets nothing at all, so roughly half of any real loadout has no suit and the design has to say so rather than leave a gap.

The constraint this is all working around

The card already spends its field colour on tier. Suit cannot also be a field colour, because two of the three pairs are nearly the same hue — and one of them is not close, it is indistinguishable. Measured as RGB distance:

Bells vs Bronze — 28.7 apart
Moons vs Silver — 92.2
Keys vs Silver — 121.9

So suit has to be carried by glyph, shape or position — colour can only reinforce it. Every option below obeys that; they differ in where the glyph goes and in what it costs.

Order the cards by

What each option costs

  • A — Suit spine. Strongest at a distance: a solid colour bar plus a glyph plus a word, down the whole edge, so a column of cards reads as stripes before you read anything. Costs about a tenth of the card's width, which comes off the condition line — the text that was already tightest. Suitless cards get a hatched neutral spine, which is honest but gives dead weight to half the loadout.
  • B — Suit medallion. Cheapest: one disc in the top row, no layout cost at all. Reads well per-card, but a grid of medallions does not form the stripes that A does, so scanning relies on the grouping rather than the card. Suitless is a dashed empty disc.
  • C — Suit is the field. Strongest possible suit read, because the whole card carries it — and the honest cost is that tier stops being glanceable, demoted to a top band and the rank pips. Worth it only if suit matters more than tier when choosing a buff, which is a game-design question, not a UI one.
  • D — Ordering only. The card is untouched; the gallery groups into labelled suit sections. Costs nothing per card and makes scanning trivial — but a card lifted out of its section, or seen in the fenced group, carries no suit signal at all.

Ordering, separately from the card

  • The toggle at the top reorders every option at once, so the two decisions can be judged independently. Suit, then tier puts the four suitless buffs in their own group at the end, which is where they belong: they are the ones a suit-led search should stop looking at.
  • Tier, then suit is what the gallery does today. It answers "what is my best buff", where suit-first answers "what pays if I go for Bells this hand" — which is closer to the question actually being asked mid-trick.

Open — none of this is mine to settle

  • Does suit outrank tier when a player picks a buff? That decides whether C is right or absurd.
  • Should the suitless buffs group first or last? Last is assumed here.
  • Every colour, width and size is a PLACEHOLDER — the spine width, the medallion diameter, the lightened suit fields in C, and the tier band's height.
  • The suit glyphs here carry a heavier stroke-width than the sheet's, because they render at ~11px. The real SuitMark.tsx sets no stroke-width at all so call sites choose — do not copy this part into src/.
