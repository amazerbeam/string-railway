The reference sheet settled what the face is, at about 11.5rem a card. This sheet asks whether it survives at --wc-card-w, which ships today as clamp(2.9rem, 6.2vmin, 4.3rem) — 46px to 69px. Drag the width slider and watch the two gauges: the plan's two open tuning values are exactly the points where they turn red. Giving 8 a dashed edge while 6 gets none tells the player 8 is special, which is the defect the ticket opens with.

What this sheet is for

  • The two gauges are the decision. At today's --wc-card-w the pip glyph is 7.7–11.5px against AC7's ~14px floor, and the name line is 3.5–5.2px. A ~14px pip needs about 5.25rem; a ~9px name needs about 7.5rem. Both are yours.
  • The name suppresses itself rather than shrinking. Below --wc-card-name-min-w a container query drops the name line instead of printing it illegibly. The threshold is a tuning value — it is 150px here to make the behaviour visible.
  • Greyscale is the AC2 check. Solid-2px vs dashed vs default, plus art present or absent, is a form distinction, so it should survive. DLR-148 is the recorded case where that claim was made and then failed, so take the screenshot rather than trusting this sentence.
  • The figures are compositional placeholders. They show the pose and the crop and how much of the face a painting gets. Real art replaces the symbol bodies with no layout change.
  • No :has(). The tooltip is :hover, :focus-within and a tap class — the ticket asked whether :has() was safe to rely on, and the answer here is that it is not needed.
  • PLACEHOLDER, yours: card width, aspect ratio, the twelve figure colours, parchment tones, grain opacity, the name threshold, and the discard-selected treatment.
