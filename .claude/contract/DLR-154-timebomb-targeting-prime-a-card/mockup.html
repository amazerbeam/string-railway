Priming a Timebomb

The one buff in the game that attaches to a card, and everything the player needs to see while it does: the hand waiting for a choice, the bomb sat on the card that took it, the row that says what is riding, and the control that takes it all back. Every size, hue and duration on this page is a placeholder.

The flow

Press Activate Timebomb, then tap a card. The mode ends on that choice. The × on the riding row — or Escape with focus in the hand — takes the Timebomb back off the trick, lifting the mark and closing the mode together.

Your lead

Riding this trick
Taker of Bells lights up 2 of your cards
Timebomb Not yet primed — pick a card in your hand.

One source of truth (AC6). Nothing on this page carries a "primed" flag of its own. The mark, the row's sentence and the remove label all read the same single primed-card value — which is why they cannot disagree, and why the row says not yet primed rather than guessing.

The mark, at hand scale

A skull replaces the art — it is what the card is for that trick. A Timebomb is added — a temporary thing done to a card that is otherwise still itself. A primed Swan is still a Swan.

  • On the wrapper, not in the card. The bomb overhangs the top-right corner, so it must hang on the card's wrapper rather than inside the card's own box.
  • Inline shapes, not <use>. A cloned <use> puts the spark in a shadow tree the fizz class cannot reach, which would leave it dead and unreachable by prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Reduced motion stops the fizz and leaves the spark lit. The mark still reads; it stops moving. "Stop the fizz" above shows the same result the media query produces.
  • Greyscale. A black silhouette on a pale card — shape alone, no hue needed.
  • The mark says nothing about tier or damage. Deliberate: priming a second Timebomb overwrites the first's tier, so a per-card figure could end up lying.

What this sheet is asking you to decide

  • Taking a Timebomb back off the trick is a rule change. As drawn, the × refunds its action points, returns the card to your pile and lifts the mark — a full undo, within the same trick window. No other activated card can be undone today.
  • The hand's waiting treatment is new. No approved sheet shows what the hand looks like while it waits; the tint, the edge and the drift above are a first proposal.
  • Copy. The prompt, the row's two sentences and the remove label are all placeholders.
  • Two loud marks on one small object. The skulled-and-primed card in the row above is the case worth judging at real scale.
