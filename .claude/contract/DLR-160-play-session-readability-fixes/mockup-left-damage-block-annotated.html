Option A, annotated

Every element numbered, with what it is, where its number comes from, and what you do with it. Then the overlaps — the same fact said twice — and the places the information is not as clear as it looks.

First: the numbers in the earlier mockup were wrong, and the error is instructive

I invented that data, and it broke the game's own rules in two places. A Key-Taker was shown firing on a Dodge — but a Taker pays for winning a trick, and a dodge is a trick you lost. And a Bell-Feeder was shown struck through as unfired on that same dodge — but a Feeder pays for losing, so on a dodge it would have paid, unless the suit did not match.

The arithmetic was wrong too: +1 then ×2 should add 2 to the total, and the row beneath claimed it added 1.

Worth saying out loud because it is the exact confusion this panel exists to prevent — and because it means a mockup's sample data has to obey the rules, or it teaches the wrong thing during review. The block below uses a corrected scenario, spelled out under the legend.

The scenario this shows

Trick 3. You held total 0, roll 0 going in. They led a 2 carrying a skull; you played a 7 and did not take it — a dodge, which banks and costs you nothing. Base damage is 1. A Sidestep (pays when you dodge a skull) adds 1. A Key-Feeder (pays when you lose a trick in Keys) doubles it. A Moon-Taker was armed and could not pay, because you did not take the trick. So the trick adds (1 + 1) × 2 = 4, the streak climbs to 1, and the pot is 4 × 1 = 4.

What each part is

1

“The pot” — the label clear

Names the block. The pot is the money on the table: what you would deal to the Quarry if you chose to cash out right now.

2

The pot figure said twice

total × roll. The single number the whole block is about, and the largest thing on it — that inversion was one of the twelve fixes, because this figure used to be set smaller than the trick's own contribution.

Source: potValue(total, roll). Also printed on the Apply button 11 — see finding 4.

3

“4 banked × 1 streak” said twice

The two figures the pot multiplies. Banked is your running damage total; streak is how many tricks in a row you have banked without being hurt.

Source: RoundState.total and RoundState.roll. Restated as a transition by row 10 — see findings 1 and 3.

4

The consequence line said twice

What just happened to your running state, in words: the streak survived and grew. It is the only line here that speaks to the outcome axis rather than the arithmetic.

Source: the existing bank meter's consequence text. The felt says the same event in different words at the same moment — see finding 8.

5

“This trick added” — the breakdown's heading

Separates the running state above from this one trick's contribution below. Past tense, deliberately: this has already happened and is already inside the pot figure. Nothing below this line is still pending.

6

Base damage clear

Every banked trick is worth 1 before anything modifies it. Shown so the other rows have something to visibly modify, rather than a number appearing from nowhere.

Source: BASE_DAMAGE.

7

A buff that paid, on the damage axis

Sidestep pays when you lose a trick that carried a skull — a dodge. It is the only condition in the game that can never fire on a bad outcome. (Blade) is the reward axis: Blade adds damage, Momentum multiplies it.

Source: a fired row from the resolution beats.

8

A buff that paid, on the multiplier axis unclear

Key-Feeder pays when you lose a trick in Keys — which a dodge is. Momentum means it multiplies rather than adds, so it applies to the sum of everything above it: (1 + 1) × 2.

This row's ×2 sits in the same column as +1, and the order it applies in is invisible — see finding 2.

9

A buff that was armed and did not pay

Struck through, with the condition it needed. This row is the whole reason the play session produced a false bug report: a Feeder correctly paid nothing, and nothing on screen said why, so it read as a broken multiplier.

Source: armed minus fired. The — means “paid nothing”, which is not quite the same as zero — see finding 7.

10

The “Banked” row said twice

Where the two running figures moved to. total 0→4 is this trick's 4 damage entering the bank; roll 0→1 is the streak ticking up.

Both destinations are already stated by 3, in different words — see findings 1 and 3.

11

Apply damage — cash out

Deals the pot to the Quarry now. Your total and streak reset to zero afterwards, so this is the moment you decide the run is worth banking rather than growing. The number on it is the pot 2.

When the pot would end the fight this control gains a lethal tag. Otherwise it never says what the Quarry can survive — see finding 6.

12

Roll over — let it ride unclear

Decline to cash out. If you bank the next trick the pot grows; if you are hurt, the whole thing goes to zero. That is the game's core gamble, and these two buttons are where it is actually made.

10+ is the floor, not a forecast — see finding 5.

What overlaps, and what is not clear

Ranked by how often a player meets them. Every trick beats once a fight; the frequency sets the order, not how annoying any one of them looks.

1. “Banked” means two different things, four lines apart

Every trick · vocabulary collision

In the header 3, “4 banked” is a noun — your running damage total. In the row label 10, “Banked” is a verb in the past tense — this trick was banked rather than hurting you. Same word, two grammatical roles, one small panel.

This is the same trap the project already documents around win and lose: one word doing duty on both the mechanical axis and the outcome axis. It is worth not repeating it with a second word.

Fix: rename one of them. The header's could read 4 damage × 1 streak, leaving “banked” to mean only the outcome. Copy is yours — but they should not both be “banked”.

2. The value column mixes addends, multipliers and nothings

Every trick with a multiplier buff · you cannot read down and sum

The column reads +1, +1, ×2, —. Three different kinds of arithmetic in one right-aligned stack, with the order of operations invisible. A player summing the column gets 2; the answer is 4.

The multiplier's scope is the real problem: ×2 applies to everything above it, and nothing on screen says so. It looks like a row, but it is an operation on the other rows.

Two ways out, differing in kind. Either group them — adds in one cluster with a subtotal, then the multipliers applied to that subtotal beneath a rule, so the arithmetic is visible as a shape; or drop the column entirely and write each row as a sentence with its own running result on the right (2, then 4), so you read a sequence rather than a sum. The first keeps the table; the second admits it is not really a table.

3. The header and the “Banked” row are the same two numbers

Every trick · redundancy

3 says total is 4 and streak is 1. 10 says total went 0→4 and roll went 0→1. The second is the first plus where it came from. One of them is carrying no new information.

Fix: keep the transition and drop the restatement, or the reverse. The transition is more useful on the trick it changes and useless on a trick that changed nothing — which argues for keeping the header stable and showing the transition only when it moved.

4. The pot figure appears twice, 200px apart

Every trick · arguably fine

The headline 2 and the Apply button 11 both read 4. I do not think this is a defect — a control that states what it will do is good practice, and the two are far enough apart to read as confirmation rather than repetition. Flagging it so the decision is deliberate rather than accidental.

5. “10+” — the plus is unexplained

Every trick · the number the gamble turns on

Roll over shows the pot you would hold after banking the next trick — but only its floor, because next trick's base damage is known while any buffs you arm are not. So the true figure is “at least 10”. The + carries all of that, and a player who has not been told will read it as “10 or so”.

Two ways out. Either say it in the hint — “at least 10, before any buff you arm” — which costs a line; or drop the number to a range or a caret glyph and let the hint carry the meaning alone. The first is clearer, the second is quieter. Both beat an unexplained plus.

6. You only learn the Quarry's health when the pot is already lethal

Every trick · asymmetric information

The lethal state names what Aoife holds. The ordinary state does not — so 4 is a number with no scale attached, and the question the button exists to answer (“is this worth cashing?”) needs a glance up to the top-right health bar and back.

Strictly the health is on screen, so this is not a hidden-information failure — it is a distance one. The two figures you are comparing sit at opposite corners.

Fix: state the target's remaining health on the Apply control in every state, not only the lethal one. Then the lethal tag becomes an emphasis of something already there rather than the only time the figure appears.

7. The dead row truncates, and its “—” is ambiguous

Only when a buff misses · minor

“Moon-Taker — needed you to take a trick with Moons” is already near this column's width, and a longer condition will clip. The — in the value column reads as “nothing” but sits where numbers live, so it can be read as zero — which is a subtly different claim (it fired and paid nothing, versus it never fired).

Fix: let the dead row wrap to two lines rather than truncating, and drop its value cell entirely — a row with no number does not need an empty numeric column.

8. The consequence line and the felt say the same event, differently

Every trick · cross-surface

Here: “Skull dodged. The streak climbs.” On the felt at the same moment: “Dodge — they took it, and it carried a skull, so it banks and costs you nothing.” Same event, two vocabularies, both visible at once.

I would leave this. They are in different zones doing different jobs — the felt explains the outcome, this line explains what it did to your running state — and the felt's version disappears when you carry on while this one persists. Worth knowing it is deliberate rather than missed.
