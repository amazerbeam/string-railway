A War Council round, mid-play, wearing the reskin

Same shell as the shipped screen — the tokens, the felt, the brass, the Iowan serif and the status/table/hand zoning are lifted from src/app/warCouncil/warCouncil.css rather than reinvented. What changed is what the pieces are. It is operable: open a stack, arm a Colour, commit it, watch the Gambit resolve, carry on.

Keyboard: Tab to the arms, ←/→ between them, Enter to open, ←/→ inside, Enter to arm and again to commit, Escape to back out.

The colours came from the app, not from me

The first pass picked oxblood, azure and a green for the three arms. On this felt (#16241f) the green was green on green. The shipped screen already has three suit hues proven against it — --wc-bells, --wc-keys, --wc-moons — so each arm's fabric now uses one of them as its lit step, darkened beneath so the numeral still reads. The reskin renames the suits; it has no business repainting them.

That also settles the collision I flagged earlier: arms take the three suit hues, which frees oxblood ↔ azure to keep doing what they already do — faction, visible on the furled Colours in the opponent's plate.

Two layouts, one state — switch top right

Zoned is the shipped shell wearing the reskin: status band, felt table with its lip, hand below. Safe, and it inherits every layout decision already made and reviewed.

Map is the idea from the design doc's open list — no panels at all. One continuous ground runs from the Marshal's encampment, across two churned front lines, through the contested middle, down to your camp. The felt's rounded rectangle stops existing; the plates lose their borders; every piece gets a contact shadow so it reads as planted rather than floating. Depth is carried by the integer sprite scale — theirs smallest, the contested pieces mid, yours largest — rather than by drawing perspective.

Both run the same DOM and the same interactions, so the comparison is only about presentation. Flip between them on the same game state.

What the map still needs before it is real

  • The terrain is CSS, not art. Hard-stepped bands and a rut pattern, standing in for drawn ground. The design doc already flags the map as a materially bigger art scope than the flat layout, and this does not discharge that — it shows the composition so you can judge the idea, not the finish.
  • It needs a name, and two obvious ones are taken. “Board” belongs to the Vanguard's hex grid; “Field” collides with Field Advantage; and “Muster” is the Vanguard's move budget — I used it as a zone label in an earlier pass and that was a mistake. Untaken candidates: the Council Ground, the Standing, the Encampment. Not picking one.
  • The Marshal's side shows furled Colours here, which is one of the two readings the doc leaves open. The map makes the case for it — an empty far side leaves a third of a continuous landscape with nothing standing on it.

What both layouts are doing

  • Three stacks, not a fan of thirteen. Whether you hold the led arm is the follow-suit question; grouping by arm makes it a glance. An exhausted arm is a dashed outline — you can see you are void.
  • Confirmation is on the Colour. One tap opens a stack (free browsing), one arms, one commits — two taps on the piece itself, no trip to a distant button.
  • Nothing a decision needs is behind hover. Stacks open on click; hover only previews the lift.
  • Committed Colours are condensed against the ones in your detachment — a played piece is a record, not a choice.
  • Every state reads without colour. Armed lifts, gains a chevron and a rule. A locked arm dims and gains a hatch. Depletion is the stack's thickness.
  • The scoring band is on screen. Humble and Victorious both pay 6; only Greedy pays nothing. If the design's argument is that winning a Gambit is not simply good, the player should be able to see where they stand.

Yours to tune, not mine to pick

Sprite scale steps (×2/×3/×4 by viewport — integer only, since fractional scaling destroys pixel art), stack overlap and fan spread, the terrain band positions, how long a resolved Gambit holds, and whether the band indicator belongs on screen at all.

Honest limits

  • This is not the engine. Resolution here is the plain rule from the rulebook — a favoured-arm Colour beats an unfavoured one, otherwise the higher value of the led arm. Abilities are not wired. src/warCouncil/ owns all of it for real.
  • I could not drive the running app. Chrome was already open on the devtools profile, so this is built from warCouncil.css and the components rather than from the live screen, and the no-scroll behaviour has not been verified at named viewport sizes. Close that Chrome window and I will check both layouts properly.
