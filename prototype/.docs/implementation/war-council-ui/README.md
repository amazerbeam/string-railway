## Responsibility

The playable **Hunt screen**: a full-viewport, non-scrolling game surface that renders a dealt
`WarCouncilState`, lets a human play a six-trick hand of it by hand against a deterministic Quarry
(a _telegraphing_ one until DLR-148 deleted the telegraph, and **partly telegraphing again since
DLR-155** — the suit it is about to lead, and nothing more),
and reports the finished round back through SCRUM-37's `WarCouncilMountProps` contract. It owns
**presentation and sequencing only** — every rules question is delegated to `src/warCouncil/` (see
[../war-council/README.md](../war-council/README.md)), and this module contains no legality check,
no scoring rule, and no trick-winner computation of its own.

DLR-53 added the Hunt layer on top of the round renderer rather than rebuilding it: persistent
readouts in a dossier column, the Quarry's intent telegraphed before every commit, and an end panel.
**The telegraph half of that was deleted outright by DLR-148 on 2026-08-26**, and **DLR-155 brought
a much narrower version back on 2026-08-31** — the lead _suit_ only, marked inside the holds panel
rather than on the felt, with no stance and no reading of a card the player has merely selected — see
[The felt rail, the trick readout, and the skull face](felt-rail-and-the-trick-readout.md). Every
number on that screen originates in `src/hunt/config.ts` and arrives already derived — see
[The dossier readouts, and the telegraph that used to sit beside them](hunt-readouts-and-telegraph.md).

**DLR-71 put the duel on screen** — two **health bars** as a mirrored opposed pair across the status
row — and `App.tsx` began carrying a real `EncounterState`, so health depleted and an encounter could
end. See [The duel's health bars](duel-health-bars.md).

**DLR-80 replaced most of this screen, and it is the largest deletion the module has taken.** Four
components and two stylesheets went: `DeclareGate.tsx`, `HuntLedger.tsx`, `StandingTrack.tsx`,
`standingSegments.ts`, `warCouncilDeclare.css` and `warCouncilStandingTrack.css`. What replaced them:

- **`QuarryShape.tsx`** — per suit, how many cards the Quarry holds and how many are skulled, and
  **never a rank**. The type it renders has no rank field, so that rule is enforced by the data.
- **`BankMeter.tsx`** — the bank, the multiplier, and what the streak would cash for. It took the
  Standing track's slot in the dossier column and the pending figure's job on the health bars, and it
  is monotonic where the pending figure was not. **PT-002 relabelled it** — the left term now reads
  "Tricks" and the right "Multiplier", the four outcome messages no longer say "Both cards banked",
  and the layout and every `.wc-bank-*` class are untouched.
- **A `skulled` prop on `PlayingCard`**, passed through by `TrickWell`, marking a skulled card once
  it is face up — with the skull in the **accessible name**, not only in the glyph.
- **A hand-over tally** in place of the scoring equation, and no Apply-the-damage control at all.

The structural change behind all of it: **the reducer now owns the live `EncounterState`** and
applies each trick's damage as the trick resolves, rather than holding a nullable applied copy while
`App.tsx` owned the real one. So the encounter can resolve **mid-hand**, and the felt renders the
terminal outcome in place of the trick well when it does.

**DLR-90 added a second consumable to the felt rail, and had to split the reducer to fit it.**
`roundReducer.ts` sat at **382 lines of a blocking 400-line budget** before a line of the feature was
written, so `roundUiState.ts` (the state, seed, actions and predicates) and `roundHint.ts`
(`deriveHint`, moved out of `WarCouncilRound.tsx`) came out first as **pure moves** — verified by every
existing spec passing with no assertion edited, only import lines. Nothing is re-exported from the old
path; all ten importers were repointed, because two valid paths to the same type is the ambiguity this
project's single-source-of-truth rule exists to prevent. On top of that: an `TimebombCharge` plate
beside the Cheat rail with the same three-tap arm and `Escape` contract, a second `primed` marker on
`PlayingCard` sharing the `skulled` prop's rendering path rather than adding a second one, and the mark
threaded to **all four** surfaces a card renders on. (All of that went with DLR-166; the reducer
split and the shared marker path are what survived.)

**DLR-91 made this module the place Timebomb is paid, without adding a single pixel to the felt.** The hit
used to be settled one layer up, at a hand boundary; it is now folded into the resolving trick's own
damage here. `applyResolution` became **pay → clear → re-book**, a `TimebombOptions` helper hands the two
pending figures and the held-Guard flag into **both** `playCard` call sites — **renamed `playOptions` by
DLR-92**, when it stopped assembling only Timebomb facts — and `blastGuardHeld` joins
`RoundUiSeed`, `RoundUiState` and both `onComplete` results — travelling the exact path `timebombCharges`
already proved. The felt shows none of it: **pending Timebomb and a held Guard are both invisible during a
fight**, which is recorded as a known tension rather than solved.

**DLR-94 added the rail's third plate, and had to split the reducer again to fit it.** `roundReducer.ts`
stood at **390 of its 400-line budget**, so the Quarry's half of a commit — `deriveResolvedTrick`,
`advanceQuarryFollow`, `advanceQuarryLead` and `CpuAdvanceResult` — moved verbatim into
**`quarryAdvance.ts`** first, the same kind of seam DLR-90 split on and a pure move with no behaviour
change. In the same pass **`canAct` moved into `roundUiState.ts`, exported**: it had been private to the
reducer while `WarCouncilRound.tsx` recomputed the identical six clauses inline as `interactive`, and two
readings of one gate is precisely how a greyed control and a reducer branch drift apart. On top of that:
an `ApplyDamagePlate` beside the Timebomb plate (the component was **deleted by DLR-114**; its
behaviour is the action bar's Apply Damage button now) with a **two-tap** poise-then-commit grammar (one boolean,
not `TimebombStage`'s two stages — its second tap _is_ the action), a reducer commit that re-asks the
refusal predicate on **both** taps so a poise cannot survive the felt changing under it, and a second
figure on `BankMeter` showing what the streak pays if the player is caught. The at-risk heart preview
deliberately still shows the full figure. **DLR-109 changed what the committing tap does — it now
spends AP and queues the payout rather than dealing it — with no `.tsx` file touched.**
**DLR-156 deleted the whole control** — the button, its five refusals, its AP cost and the queue
behind it — and replaced it with a second full-viewport screen raised after every resolved trick.
See [the trick resolution screen](the-resolution-screen.md).

It sits under `src/app/` rather than beside the engine for a hard reason: `eslint.config.js`'s
pure-core override bars `src/warCouncil/**` from importing React at all, so a `.tsx` file there
would trip `no-restricted-imports` (the same override previously also scoped `src/vanguard/**`
before DLR-47 deleted that tree). `src/app/` is the layer that is _expected_ to consume the engine
and import React, which makes this its natural home. See [../app/README.md](../app/README.md) for
the mount-prop contract this module implements.

The folder deliberately has **no barrel**. `App.tsx` imports the mount directly by path
(`./app/warCouncil/WarCouncilRound`); `src/app/index.ts` excludes components on purpose, and a `.ts`
barrel re-exporting one is a needless brush with `react-refresh/only-export-components`.

**DLR-100 added a fourth felt-rail plate, and the one genuinely new gate in this module: a control
available while `canAct` is false.** `DiscardPlate` sat beside `ApplyDamagePlate` (both **deleted by
DLR-114**, their behaviour now the action bar's Swap and Apply Damage buttons), a sibling of the
other three consumable controls rather than a generalisation of any — the codebase's own stated
reason for keeping them separate ("retuning one never risks the others") applies again. What makes
it structurally different from its three siblings: Cheat, Timebomb and Apply Damage all gate on
`canAct`, which requires `currentTurn === PlayerSide.Player`. The discard must also open **before
the Quarry's own lead** — the gap where `canAct` is false because the Quarry, not the player, is
next to move, but the trick has not started — so `WarCouncilRound.tsx` computes a **second**
`interactive` value, `handInteractive = interactive || discardSelecting(ui) || timebombArmed(ui)`
(the third term is DLR-154's), passed to `HandFan` alone; every other consumer of `interactive` (`CheatSlots` and `TimebombCharge`, both of which
DLR-114 relocated inside the buff panel and DLR-132 then deleted outright) keeps reading the unchanged value. `HandFan` gained a third "every card is a valid tap target" mode
alongside `timebombArmed`, added as a second clause in the same two expressions rather than a parallel
branch, and `PlayingCard` gained a third per-card marker following `skulled`/`primed`'s own
rendering path. A mid-implementation defect — the new reducer branch sitting behind the `canAct`
guard rather than ahead of it, which let the rail open pre-lead but swallowed every attempt to
select a card inside that same gap — was found and fixed before review, and a post-review guard on
`handleCarryOn` stops a felt-background tap from silently dropping an open selection during the same
window. See
[The discard plate, the hand's third mode, and the pre-lead gate](discard-plate-and-selection.md).

**DLR-114 retired the felt rail's four plates and replaced them with one action bar, and it is the
largest interaction change this module has taken since DLR-80.** `ApplyDamagePlate.tsx`,
`DiscardPlate.tsx` and their two stylesheets and two specs were deleted; `CheatSlots` and
`TimebombCharge` were **relocated**, unchanged, into a new `BuffLoadoutPanel` (**deleted by
DLR-148** — the panel is `BuffGallery` now, and both relocated controls had already gone on
DLR-132); and `.wc-felt-rail` keeps only `DecreePile` (**widened by DLR-148** into the felt's whole
left column). In their place `ActionBar` renders four controls — Apply Buff, Cards, Swap,
Apply Damage — as the shell's own **fourth grid row**, always mounted for the whole hand and greying
with its reason on each control's own face rather than disappearing. Behind it, `RunState.buffs`
reaches the card layer for the first time and the felt's **two** competing AP numbers became one:
`RoundUiState.apPool` was deleted and replaced by `buffActivation: BuffActivationState`, so the pool
Apply Damage spends from and the pool `activateBuff` spends from are the same pool. See
[The action bar, the buff loadout, and the AP pool that stopped being two numbers](action-bar-and-loadout.md).

**DLR-145 deleted that pool from every surface in this module.** `AP_ENABLED` is `false`, so the
figure the action bar and the loadout panel had been rendering since DLR-114 is a resource the
player no longer has. Three things went, at source rather than by being handed a zero — because a
control reading "0 AP" still claims a resource exists: the Apply Buff button's figure
(`{apPool} AP · N held` → `N held`), the Apply Damage button's (`{cash} for N AP` → `cash {N}`), and
the loadout panel's header (`N action points left` → `N cards`, which is the panel's own scarcity
signal now that a card is spent rather than rented). `buffLine` and `buffRowAccessibleName` **lost
their `apCost` parameter entirely**, `applyBuffAccessibleName` and `applyDamageBarAccessibleName`
lost their AP clauses, and `BuffLoadoutPanelProps.apCostFor`, `BuffLoadoutPanelProps.activation` and
`ActionBarProps.apPool` were removed along with their suppliers in `roundControlsProps.ts`.
`.wc-loadout-ap` was renamed `.wc-loadout-count`. **`RoundUiState.buffActivation` itself stayed** —
its `apPool` and `capacity` are still there, still seeded, still spent from at zero cost. The one
addition in this module is in `buffRoundState.ts`: both `buffHandInputFor` and `firedOncePerHandIds`
now read `offeredBuffs ∪ buffActivation.spentThisTrick`, because a single-use condition card has
left the pile by the time the trick resolves. **No browser pass has been run** against any of it;
the loadout panel in particular now renders around twenty-one rows where it was laid out against
five. **DLR-148 is the answer to that**: the twenty-one rows became a grid of cards with duplicates
collapsed, which is a materially smaller thing to read.

**DLR-148 rebuilt the buff surface, re-homed the felt's game state, and deleted the intent
telegraph — the largest change to this screen's shape since DLR-114.** Three things happened
together because they share the felt's grid:

- **`BuffLoadoutPanel` is gone, replaced by a gallery of buff _cards_.** `BuffGallery.tsx`,
  `BuffCard.tsx`, `BuffRunTab.tsx` and `BuffTierFilter.tsx` render a `BuffGalleryView` built by a
  new pure module, `buffGalleryModel.ts`: runs grouped by target suit (with `Press` and `Suitless`
  as the fourth and fifth), tier-descending order, exact duplicates collapsed into a counted pile,
  and everything unusable right now fenced into one tarnished group carrying the count and the
  shared reason. **The activation gate did not move** — the fence reads `loadoutRefusalFor`, which
  reads `buffActivationWindowOpen`, exactly as the old panel's rows did. `Escape` now unwinds **one
  level**, which is the one genuinely new interaction: a new `CancelBuffPoise` action drops an
  unspent poise and leaves the panel open, while `CancelLoadout` keeps meaning "close outright".
- **The felt became `game rail | stage`.** `.wc-table`'s `1fr auto 1fr` with an absolutely
  positioned panel over the middle is now two grid columns: `FeltRail.tsx`, always mounted, carrying
  decree, the trick and its readout and the spent pile; and `FeltStage.tsx`, carrying the gallery or
  the felt's narrative states. Non-occlusion is a **structural** guarantee now rather than a
  z-index promise. The felt's branch chain moved verbatim into `roundControlsProps.ts`'s
  `feltStageProps`, taking `WarCouncilRound.tsx` from **415 lines (already in breach)** to 352.
- **A consequence readout replaced the telegraph.** `trickConsequenceModel.ts` derives, from the
  Quarry's led card alone, what taking the trick does to the player and what not taking it does —
  and returns `null`, so nothing renders at all, when the trick is empty, when the _player_ led, or
  when the card has nothing extra to say. `IntentTelegraph.tsx`, `intentPreview.ts`,
  `previewQuarryIntent`, `intentAccessibleName` and `STANCE_PHRASE` are deleted with their three
  specs. **`quarryIntent`, `TelegraphFidelity` and `TELEGRAPH_FIDELITY` deliberately stay in the
  engine with no production consumer** — a recorded decision, not an oversight. **(DLR-155 gave
  `quarryIntent` a consumer again — `telegraphedLeadSuit`, for the holds panel's lead mark. The
  deleted preview surface above stayed deleted.)**

In the same pass a skulled card gained a **skull face** in place of its corner glyph (identical for
every rank and suit, corner index intact), and `__tests__/contrast.test.ts` began enforcing a 4.5:1
WCAG floor by parsing the real stylesheet. See [The buff gallery](buff-gallery.md) and
[The felt rail, the trick readout, and the skull face](felt-rail-and-the-trick-readout.md).

**DLR-150 put the Feeder carry on the felt and split two files to make room.** `BankMeter` gained
`carriedIn` and `carryOut` — display-only `BuffCarry` props, folded into the section's existing
`aria-label` rather than added as landmarks, and deliberately **not** folded into `cash`/`forced`,
because the carry pays nothing in the hand that earns it.
`WarCouncilRound.tsx` forwards a `lowCarry` mount prop into the `useReducer` seed and hands the
hand's `accrual.carryOut` back out. Two extractions came with it, both forced by the 400-line budget
and both collapsing real duplication: **`roundResult.ts`**, holding `roundResultFor(ui)` — the one
construction site of `WarCouncilRoundResult`, replacing two literals here and a third hand-built copy
in `src/sim/playHand.ts` — and **`roundUiSeed.ts`**, holding `RoundUiSeed` and `createRoundUiState`,
re-exported from `roundUiState.ts` so no importer had to change. The `.wc-bank*` rules moved to a new
`warCouncilBankMeter.css` for the same reason. See
[The readouts](hunt-readouts-and-telegraph.md#both-halves-of-the-low-carry--dlr-150-2026-08-27)
and, for the mechanic, [hunt/the-low-carry.md](../hunt/the-low-carry.md).

**DLR-156 gave this module a second screen, and split the mount to make room for it.**
`WarCouncilRound.tsx` stood at 390 of its 400-line budget and could not absorb one, so it became a
**99-line switch** that owns the reducer and nothing else: it renders `WarCouncilTable` (the felt,
the hand, the action bar — moved out wholesale, with no behaviour change) while `ui.resolution` is
`null`, and `TrickResolutionScreen` the moment a trick resolves. Four new files came with it —
`resolutionBeats.ts` (pure, the build-up derived from what the engine decided), `resolutionLabels.ts`
(its wording), `useBeatSequence.ts` (the clock) and `useCardFlight.ts` (the played card's travel from
hand to table — **generalised into `useCardMotion.ts` and renamed by DLR-157**; `useCardFlight` no
longer exists) — plus `ResolutionLedger.tsx` and `warCouncilResolve.css`. **The Apply Damage button
left the action bar entirely**, taking `applyDamageStock`, `applyPoised`, the
`TapApplyDamage`/`CancelApplyDamage` pair, `payoutLabels.ts` and the delayed-payout queue with it.
See [the trick resolution screen](the-resolution-screen.md).

**DLR-160 answered one 33-minute play session, and twelve of its findings landed on this module.**
Not one of them was a rule change: every frustration in that session was something that could not be
seen, could not be reached, or was lost to a stray click. The consequential half is that **the trick
no longer moves on without the player** — the felt's whole-region click is gone, and the resolution
panel's two exits stopped laying the Quarry's lead in the same dispatch that dismissed them, which
had been closing the between-tricks arming window before the player ever saw the felt again
([interaction and state](interaction-and-state.md#dlr-160--advancing-the-trick-is-an-explicit-press-and-only-an-explicit-press)).
The rest is what the screen now says: **which of the four outcomes a trick was, and why**, from one
module the trick well and the resolution panel both read
([reading a resolved trick](reading-a-resolved-trick.md)); which armed buffs paid nothing, and what
they needed; the decree; and whether the pot would end the fight. The resolution surface stopped
being a second full-viewport screen and became **a corner panel over a still-mounted felt**, which
means the table is no longer torn down and remounted once per trick. Four files split under the
400-line budget in the process — `resolutionView.ts`, `warCouncilResolvePanel.css`,
`ResolutionBreakdown.tsx` and `BuffRideZone.tsx` — all pure moves. **Almost every value it ships is a
placeholder;** the one figure the developer set is `--wc-trick-dwell`, at 1000ms.

The zone components — `RoundStatusBand`, `DecreePile`, `TrickWell`, `HandFan`, `AbilityPrompt`,
`RoundOverPanel` — are each a default export consumed only by `WarCouncilRound.tsx`. DLR-53 added
three more in the same shape: `QuarryDossier` and `IntentTelegraph` (both mounted in the `wc-dossier`
zone), and a ledger since deleted — **`IntentTelegraph` was itself deleted by DLR-148**, leaving
`QuarryDossier` alone in that zone. **DLR-148 also inserted two new zone components above the felt's
own chain**: `FeltRail` and `FeltStage`, the two grid columns `.wc-table` became. **DLR-71 added `DuelHealthBars`**, mounted inside
`RoundStatusBand` with the `You · Trick · Them` trio passed as its `centre` prop.

**DLR-100 gave `HandFan` a third "every card is a valid tap target" mode.** `timebombArmed` already
relaxed `isFocusable` and the `illegal` expression so a card illegal to play stays tappable while
marking; `discardSelecting` is a second clause added beside it in both expressions, not a parallel
branch, for the identical reason — discarding is not a move either. The one genuinely new wrinkle:
`HandFan`'s `interactive` prop must read `true` during the Quarry-to-lead window whenever
`discardSelecting` is `true`, even though `canAct(ui)` — the value every other consumer of
`interactive` reads — is `false` there. `WarCouncilRound.tsx` computes `handInteractive = interactive
|| discardSelecting(ui) || timebombArmed(ui)` (DLR-154 added the third term) and passes it to
`HandFan` alone; `CheatSlots` and `TimebombCharge` — since
DLR-114 mounted inside the buff panel rather than on the felt rail, and DLR-132 then deleted both —
kept reading the bare `interactive`.

**DLR-80 emptied the dossier column and refilled it.** `HuntLedger`, `StandingTrack` and
`DeclareGate` were deleted; `QuarryShape` and `BankMeter` took their slot, mounted directly by
`WarCouncilRound` beside the surviving `QuarryDossier` and `IntentTelegraph` (the latter deleted in
turn by DLR-148). `RoundStatusBand` kept
`bars` and gained nothing, but its trick counter now clamps against `HAND_SIZE` rather than a
hard-coded `13`.

`WarCouncilRound` supplies `QuarryShape`'s rows as `suitShape(ui.round.hands[Cpu],
ui.round.skulledCards)` and `BankMeter`'s figures straight off `ui.round` — **the same values the
engine wrote**, so a readout cannot disagree with the state it describes.

`labels.ts`, `cardFace.ts`, `roundReducer.ts`, `roundUiState.ts`, `roundHint.ts`, `handOrder.ts`,
`duelHealthBars.ts` and — since DLR-148 — `buffGalleryModel.ts`, `trickConsequenceModel.ts` and
`consequenceLabels.ts` import no React and touch no DOM global, so all ten are unit-tested in the
cheap `node` Vitest project (`intentPreview.ts` was a member of that set until DLR-148 deleted it); the components are tested in the `dom` project (see [Testing](testing.md)).

`duelHealthBars.ts` sits here rather than in the lint-enforced pure core for the same reason
`handOrder.ts` does: **how a bar is drawn is not a game rule.** It converts three health records into
percentages and performs no clamp, no rounding and no damage arithmetic of its own.

`handOrder.ts` sits here rather than in the lint-enforced pure core on purpose: **display order is not
a game rule.** DLR-148's `buffGalleryModel.ts` and `trickConsequenceModel.ts` make the same call for
the same reason — which run a card sits in and which sentences a led card produces are presentation,
not adjudication. All of them are React-free and DOM-free, but review-enforced rather than
lint-enforced. **This contract added no file to `src/hunt/**` or `src/warCouncil/**`**, so the
pure-core ESLint override's `files` array is untouched. Sorting `RoundState.hands` instead would have changed what
`dealRound` returns, and what every engine spec asserts, for a purely presentational reason.

### DLR-82 removed this module's terminal state, and inverted two problems at once

The felt's render chain used to open `if (encounterOver) { … }`, **ahead of** the resolved-trick
branch. Two consequences followed, and both were defects nobody had named: the trick that ended a
fight **never got its reveal** (the old code's own comment said so), and the player landed on a
tally table with **no control on it** — the dead end that produced the play-session feedback.

DLR-82 deleted that branch. A resolved encounter now falls through to the deciding trick's ordinary
`TrickWell` reveal, and the tap that clears it reaches `handleCarryOn`, **whose first line already
tested `encounterOver` and called `onComplete`**. The report upward therefore needed no new code
path and — the part that mattered — **no effect**: it stays a user tap, not a `useEffect` that
StrictMode would fire twice. One tap replaced two, and the deciding trick gained a beat it had
never had.

One gap the deletion opened was closed in the same task: an encounter resolving with nothing held
and the hand not complete would have rendered a felt with no branch to carry a tap. The felt's
existing waiting affordance was widened to cover it — both the `wc-is-waiting` class and the
`onClick` on `.wc-table` read `ui.resolvedTrick || quarryToLead || encounterOver`, so a tap always
had somewhere to land.

> **DLR-160 deleted both of those**, because the same region click was committing the Quarry's lead
> on any stray click in the play area. `TrickWell`'s own explicit button is now the only way to
> advance, and the reducer branches on `isEncounterResolved` before clearing a held trick so a
> mid-hand kill still has a control that reaches `onComplete`. See
> [interaction and state](interaction-and-state.md#dlr-160--advancing-the-trick-is-an-explicit-press-and-only-an-explicit-press).

`RoundOverPanel` reverted to the between-hands tally it now solely is: its `winner` prop is
**removed from the type** (so the compiler found the one production call site still passing
`winner={null}`), its heading is the constant `The hand is over`, and `ENCOUNTER_OUTCOME` and the
`.wc-terminal` rule were deleted with the branch that was their only reader.

## Rules & invariants enforced

- **This module re-implements no rule.** `legalMoves` decides what `HandFan` renders as tappable,
  `playCard` decides what commits, `chooseCpuMove` and `commitQuarryMove` play the opponent,
  `quarryIntent` computed the telegraphed stance until DLR-148 deleted every consumer of it, and —
  **since DLR-80** — `resolveTrickBank` decides
  every trick's outcome, `suitShape` builds the shape rows, and `isSkulled` decides whether a played
  card is marked. The screen reads `ui.round.lastResolution` and formats it; it never re-derives what
  a trick did. The one arithmetic a component still performs is `BankMeter`'s `bank × multiplier`,
  which is a **display preview with no rule attached** — the cash-out that actually lands is
  `resolveTrickBank`'s. Card equality is always the engine's own `sameCard`/`containsCard` (exported
  from `src/warCouncil/index.ts` by this ticket rather than deep-imported or re-written).
  `roundReducer.ts` contains no suit comparison, no rank comparison, and no trick-winner
  computation. The single permitted rank _identity_ check is "is this rank `CardRank.Fox` or
  `CardRank.Woodcutter`", for opening the ability prompt — via `CardRank`, never a numeric literal.
- **No buff firing rule is derived in this module** (DLR-153). Every figure on the lit hand, the
  riding list and the per-card breakdown comes from `projectBuffBranches`, which answers the
  question by calling `firedBuffs` and `resolveFiredBuffs` — the two functions the real trick
  resolution calls. `buffRideModel.ts` and `buffBreakdownModel.ts` hold no `switch` over
  `BuffConditionKind` and perform no accrual arithmetic; the only subtraction anywhere is a delta
  between two accruals the projection returned. The DLR-147 mockup's re-derivation reported +6
  damage for a load whose ceiling was +4, which is the failure this rule exists to prevent.
- ~~**No `useEffect` or `useLayoutEffect` anywhere**~~ — **the reducer still holds no effect**, which
  is the claim that actually matters (see
  [Interaction and state § The module has no effect at all](interaction-and-state.md)), but the
  module-wide absence has not been true for some time. Four effects existed before DLR-157, in three
  files, and **every one of them exists only to release something**: two in `WarCouncilRound.tsx`
  (the debug state mirror and its teardown), one in `useCardTip.ts` (DLR-149's tooltip listeners),
  and one in `useBuffBreakdownTarget.ts` (DLR-153), whose entire body is a cleanup clearing the hover
  bridge's `setTimeout` so StrictMode's double mount cannot leave an orphan that closes the panel
  under the next mount. **DLR-157 added two more, and they keep the same discipline**:
  `useCardMotion.ts`'s mount effect, whose whole body is a cleanup tearing down every live flight's
  timers, clones, animations and `visibilitychange` listener; and `useCardMotionDriver.ts`'s, which
  starts no timer of its own and owns nothing to release — it reads `ui.round`, diffs, and hands the
  work to the primitive whose cleanup already covers it. `useLayoutEffect` genuinely appears
  nowhere.
- **Exactly one card-motion implementation exists** (DLR-157). Every movement in the game goes
  through `useCardMotion.move`; `useCardFlight`, the `CardFlight` interface and `fly(from, to,
onLanded)` are gone, and the only `document.querySelector` the flight ever used is replaced by the
  anchor registry. `cardPlacement.ts` and `cardMotionPlan.ts` import no React and touch no DOM —
  grep-verified — and sit under `src/app/` deliberately, so the lint-enforced pure core never learns
  what a place on a screen is. **This contract added no file under `src/warCouncil/**` or
  `src/hunt/**`**, so that ESLint override's `files` array is untouched.
- **No motion value is a literal anywhere outside one `:root` block** (DLR-157). All six —
  `--wc-flight`, `--wc-flight-stagger`, `--wc-flight-lift`, `--wc-flight-tilt`, `--wc-flight-ease`,
  `--wc-flip-at` — are declared once in `warCouncilMotion.css` and read by `cardMotionConfig.ts`
  alone, each guarded so an unparseable or deleted token yields a documented fallback rather than a
  `NaN` duration Web Animations would turn into a never-finishing animation.
- **A slot mid-movement loses its paint, never its box** (DLR-157). `.wc-is-in-flight` is
  `visibility: hidden` and nothing else; `display: none` would remove the box and reflow the row
  under the player's pointer. A gap closes after a departure lands and a slot fills after an arrival
  lands, never during either.
- **`useMotionAnchors()` throws outside a `MotionAnchorProvider`** rather than returning a null
  registry that would make every movement silently instant (DLR-157). The two providers — one in
  `WarCouncilRound.tsx`, one in `ShopPanel.tsx` — are separate registries on purpose; the round's
  diff driver has nothing to say about a shop purchase.
- **`labels.ts`, `cardFace.ts`, and `roundReducer.ts` import no React and touch no DOM global** —
  verified by grep, which is what lets them run in the `node` Vitest project.
- **No component sees a numeric literal standing in for a tunable** (DLR-53, extended by every ticket
  since). Every figure arrives already derived from `src/hunt/config.ts` through the engine.
  **DLR-80 closed the last hole in that claim**: `RoundStatusBand.tsx` had clamped its trick counter
  with a hard-coded `13`, and it now reads `HAND_SIZE`. Grep-verified in the contract's final
  verification, but structural rather than merely observed: there is no path by
  which a component could invent one. See
  [The dossier readouts](hunt-readouts-and-telegraph.md).
- **Every visual value is a named CSS custom property**, transcribed from the developer-approved
  `mockup.html`. (This once read "…or a named constant in `fanLayout.ts`"; that module is gone and
  the hand's own geometry is now a `gap` and a `padding` in `warCouncilHand.css`.) No hex colour appears in any `.tsx` — a grep
  enforces this — and no `vh`/`vw` unit appears anywhere in `src/` or `index.html`; dimensions are
  `dvh`, `%`, `rem`, or `vmin`.
- **No `memo`, `useMemo`, or `useCallback`.** There is no profiling evidence for any and the
  `react-frontend` skill forbids speculative memoisation. The heaviest per-tap work is one
  `legalMoves` over at most 13 cards plus one `playCard`; every collection is bounded by the 33-card
  deck.
- No lint rule is suppressed anywhere in the module, there is no `any`, no module-level mutable
  state, and no `console.log`/`console.debug`.
- The three SVG `<symbol>` ids (`s-bells`, `s-keys`, `s-moons`) are defined in `SuitMark.tsx`'s
  sheet and consumed through the `SUIT_SYMBOL_ID` map, which **DLR-149 moved into its own
  `suitSymbolIds.ts`** — `SuitMark.tsx` is a component file and `react-refresh/only-export-components`
  rejects a non-component export beside it (the rule was not suppressed). Those two places are still
  the only two where a suit's symbol id may be written; the card face's pip lattice imports the same
  map rather than re-deriving the string. **DLR-149's eight `wc-fig-*` figure ids in
  `CardArtSheet.tsx` inherit the same hazard** and are consumed only through `RANK_FACE`'s
  `FigureKey`, which the type checker does constrain. They bind by string: a rename
  type-checks cleanly and renders nothing. **DLR-86's `HeartMark.tsx` inherits the rule verbatim**
  for `hp-heart` and `hp-heart-broken` via `HEART_SYMBOL_ID`; a mistyped id there renders an empty
  `<svg>` with no console error anywhere.
- **A pure `.ts` view-model may not share a name with its `.tsx` component up to case** (DLR-148;
  hit again, differently, by DLR-149 — `CardFace.tsx` beside `cardFace.ts` makes `tsc` fail outright
  with **TS1149** on this case-insensitive filesystem, so the component file is `CardFacePanel.tsx`
  while the exported component is still named `CardFace`).
  This toolchain's Vite/Vitest module resolution folds two source files whose names differ only by
  case into **one cached module id**, even though the filesystem underneath is case-sensitive, so
  `import BuffGallery from './BuffGallery'` silently resolved to the _model's_ exports — which have
  no default export — and the component rendered as `undefined`. It is not a test-only artefact:
  the same collision would recur in the app bundle. `buffGallery.ts` and `trickConsequence.ts` were
  renamed **`buffGalleryModel.ts`** and **`trickConsequenceModel.ts`** for this reason, and the
  `Model` suffix is the convention. A reject condition, not a preference — and a **third**
  string-bound surface of the same family as the two below, except that this one binds by _filename_
  and fails silently at render rather than at the type checker.
- **The 4.5:1 WCAG contrast floor is enforced against the real stylesheet** (DLR-148).
  `__tests__/contrast.test.ts` reads `warCouncil.css` with `readFileSync`, extracts token hexes and
  computes the relative-luminance ratio for every payoff-bar ink on its suit and every readout ink
  on the readout ground. The CSS stays the **single owner** of the colour — mirroring the hexes in
  TypeScript was rejected as the two-sources-of-truth failure this project is organised against —
  and `token()` throws on a missing token so a rename fails loudly rather than comparing `undefined`
  against `undefined`. Every colour and size DLR-148 added is a transcribed placeholder the
  developer owns; **this floor is not.**
- **The hand's layout contract is pinned against the real stylesheet**, by
  `__tests__/handRowCss.test.ts` (6 tests, the DLR-149 follow-up). It replaced `fanLayout.test.ts`
  when `fanLayout.ts` was deleted: the claims that spec made about the hand did not disappear, they
  moved into CSS, so the spec followed them there. It reads `warCouncilHand.css` and
  `warCouncilCards.css` from disk — the pattern `cardFaceCss.test.ts` established, and the only one
  available, since jsdom has no layout engine and never loads these files. It asserts that the gap
  exists and is sized off `--wc-card-w`; that the gap **exceeds `(scale − 1) / 2`**, so an armed
  card can never reach its neighbour; that the top padding covers the armed lift plus the scale
  overflow; that nothing rotates at rest and neither `--wc-fan-rot` nor `--wc-fan-lift` survives;
  that all three interaction lifts are still there; and that the armed `z-index` is positive while
  no slot creates a competing stacking context.
- **No SVG filter over `feTurbulence`, and no `mix-blend-mode`, anywhere in this module** (DLR-148).
  Both are measured performance traps rather than style preferences: the first timed out
  `Page.captureScreenshot` at 120s with eleven cards on screen, the second forces a compositing
  layer per card. The paper-grain texture they were built for is deliberately not ported, and its
  two prohibitions are recorded as CSS comments where a future ticket would add it.
- **The trick's cards render in exactly one place at a time** (DLR-148). Gallery closed, the stage's
  `TrickWell` holds them; gallery open, the rail's condensed strip does, and `FeltRailProps.trick`
  is `null` in the first case. The approved mockup rendered both at once; that duplication is the
  one place the port deliberately deviates, because two simultaneous renderings of one fact is the
  failure mode this project's single-source-of-truth rule names.
- **`HeartState`'s five values are a second string-bound surface of the same kind** (four until
  DLR-101 added `ticking`), written straight into the DOM as `data-state` and matched by attribute
  selectors in `warCouncilHealthBars.css`. The `as const` map and the stylesheet are the only two
  places they may be written — including the camelCase in `atRisk`. A rename type-checks cleanly and
  renders an unstyled heart. **DLR-115 added a second, orthogonal string-bound surface beside it**:
  `PipType`'s two values, written as `data-type`, with the stylesheet selecting on the **product** of
  the two. Every pre-existing `.wc-hp-heart[data-state=…]` selector was re-qualified with
  `[data-type='health']` in the same pass, so a shield pip cannot inherit a red rule by accident. The
  same `hp-shield` symbol-id rule applies as for `hp-heart`: two places, and a mistyped id renders an
  empty `<svg>` with no console error anywhere. **`HeartState` keeps exactly its original five
  members** — a `game-ux` ruling on DLR-115, not an oversight — so a sixth peer state is a reject
  condition here, not merely an absence.
- **`duelHealthBars`'s overlays arrive as a named object, never as positional records** (DLR-101).
  `breaking` and `ticking` are both `Readonly<Record<DuelSide, Damage>>`, so as positional arguments
  a transposition would have type-checked and drawn a plausible but wrong picture — `streak.ts`'s
  `TrickFacts` exists for the same reason. A future overlay is a field on `HealthBarOverlays`, not a
  fifth parameter — **DLR-115 was the first ticket to have to honour that**, adding `shield` as a
  third named field.
- **The absorption order is never restated in this module** (DLR-115). Both new call sites —
  `projectedDepletion` and `duelHealthBars` — delegate to `absorbWithShield` in `src/hunt/shield.ts`,
  DLR-110's single statement of "blue hearts take damage before red, one point each". Two calls, one
  rule. Threading one call's `ShieldAbsorption` into the other was rejected: it would put a derived
  engine value in a render-geometry function's argument list and make the caller responsible for
  keeping the two in step.
- **`DuelHealthBars.tsx` writes no inline style at all, and that is a guarantee rather than an
  omission.** Until DLR-86 it split bar geometry through a `--w` custom property specifically because
  an inline `width` out-ranks an external rule carrying no `!important`, which would have silently
  defeated the stylesheet's own transition and lethal-state rules. A row of fixed-size glyphs has no
  per-element geometry to communicate, so DLR-86 **designed the hazard out** rather than continuing to
  guard against it — and the component's own spec asserts that no `.wc-hp-heart` carries a `style`
  attribute. Any future need for a per-heart value must come back through a custom property.

- **✅ The short-viewport clipping defect is fixed (DLR-71), and the fix has a visual cost the
  developer owns.** DLR-67's end panel grew a second equation, overflowed the felt inside the
  stylesheet's own `@media (max-width: 44rem), (max-height: 34rem)` breakpoint, and the fix applied
  there — `justify-content: center` on an `overflow-y: auto` container — clipped content
  **symmetrically** while `scrollTop` cannot go negative, so the declare gate's "Play to Win" heading
  became unreachable at any scroll position at 680×520 and 700×544, and a click on it failed on first
  attempt. DLR-71 took the second of the two candidate resolutions: **`justify-content: flex-start`**,
  so `scrollTop: 0` shows the true top of content. It had to, rather than choosing to — DLR-71's end
  panel is taller again (two equations, two bars, a control) and its own AC gated on 1024×640 and
  phone portrait. QA re-measured both original sizes: the heading is fully visible at `scrollTop: 0`

  attempt. The cost is that the felt's content now **top-aligns rather than centring** at these two
  sizes, which is a visible change and the developer's to accept or reject — the alternative remains
  scoping the stretch/scroll to the end-panel state alone, which is more CSS and leaves centring

  test passing each time. That check belongs to QA driving the app in a real browser at named sizes,
